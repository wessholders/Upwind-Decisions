# Close windward mark approach

**Bear away to mark** uses the buoy as the aiming reference. The boat still finishes across the finite line extending upwind from that mark, on starboard, crossing right to left. A valid finish leaves the mark to port. Steering no longer targets the gate midpoint.

The shared `closestMarkApproach()` function finds the crossing nearest the mark whose approach line both clears a circular hull/buoy envelope and is legal on the current tack. A quadratic gives the interval of clear crossings. Two linear wind-angle half-planes restrict it to the current tack and no-go angle. The nearest point in the intersection is the target. It works for a rotated gate, for a boat below or above the mark, and for small bear-away adjustments such as 2°. If there is no safe legal direct approach, the selected mode remains armed and holds close-hauled; it does not invent a tack.

The training envelope is 0.6 boatlengths from boat center to mark center: a 0.5-boatlength circular hull bound plus a 0.1-boatlength buoy allowance. Navigation targets a 0.65-boatlength clearance to provide integration margin. With the default 5 m boat, those are 3.0 m required clearance and 3.25 m nominal approach clearance. This is a simplified circular model, not a full hull/contact or rounding-penalty simulation. The visible outer ring at the mark is a location aid, not the physical buoy radius.

The target crossing can be slightly farther up the line than the clearance radius, depending on approach angle. Once acquired, its distance along the gate is retained while the approach remains clear and legal. Repeatedly selecting the nearest tangent after passing the closest point switches tangent branches and bends the boat back toward the buoy. The retained target prevents this last-second tightening. If the wind or gate moves, the target is revalidated against the actual forward segment and no-go angle, and recalculated when necessary. The acquired target is part of the replayable boat state; cancelling the mode or tacking clears it. A dotted approach guide shows the current legal target. Near the final crossing, RK2 integration keeps its predicted midpoint on the approach side so it cannot look past the finish and turn back before recording the crossing.

Finish detection checks the actual final movement segment, ending at its intersection with the finish, against the hull/buoy envelope. It does not extend the heading backward or beyond the finish: that could invent a contact for a boat that has already passed safely on a curved approach. This is a local crossing check, not historical contact tracking. The player, live computer's current-picture forecast and hindsight optimizer use the same rule. The live computer also waits for a clearance-safe starboard approach before its final tack. Existing time/distance test routes include clearance; the tack penalty, boat speed response and required finish tack/direction are unchanged.

Tests cover 2° bear-away trajectories in uniform, traveling and organic wind; overstood recovery; rotated wind/gates and 30°/45°/60° close-hauled settings; nearest feasible targets; swept approach clearance; port-side passage; rejection of crossings through the buoy envelope; and acceptance of a safe curved crossing whose extended heading would falsely suggest contact. A 450-approach regression sweep spans all three wind models, 1/5/20 kn boat speeds, near-mark and distant starts, reaching and overstood approaches. Every initially legal approach must retain its target, keep its swept clearance and finish. The no-go zone, tack control, independent live observations and deterministic command replay remain shared.
