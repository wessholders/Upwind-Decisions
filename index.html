Wind conditions on the course

Puffs and light-air pockets move with their own wind. The favored side has a statistical advantage in pressure and shifts, with 65/35 odds by default. Both you and the reference route use this same field.

Outside puffs and pockets, wind stays directly upwind (0° relative to the course). Persistent and oscillating shifts apply inside features, with smooth edges returning to the ambient direction.

Boat pressure response is a simplified training rule: speed = base boat speed × (local wind / background wind)response. The default 0.5 uses a square-root response; 0 keeps boat speed fixed. This is not a calibrated boat polar.

Uniform persistent shifts use seeded start, direction, magnitude and duration. Traveling persistent shifts use the angle, entry start and width above. Traveling oscillations use seeded targets and holds with the selected transition width.

Traveling wind is a simplified training model: horizontal fronts move down the course at the pattern speed. This is separate from boat speed and actual wind speed. Dashed fronts show transition centers; streak angles show local wind direction. Laylines and the gate follow wind at the mark.
